How might AI replace human workers?

Before answering the big question, we have to figure out what we actually need to know. So this is everything I found out about workers, costs, AI "employees" and where the economy might be going. Spoiler: it's complicated, and kind of scary.

IN 08:00

Human workers & traditional jobs

First we need to understand who is actually working right now, and which parts of their jobs are things only a person can do.

  • Which jobs are mostly repetitive?Cashiers, call centers, data entry, a lot of office paperwork. If you can write the job as a list of steps, a computer can probably learn it.
  • What can humans do that AI still can't?Empathy, common sense, reading a room, fixing a pipe in some weird old bathroom, and being responsible when something goes wrong.
  • How many people around us could be affected?In Argentina a lot of people work informally or in small family shops. Would AI even reach them the same way it reaches a big company in the US? I honestly don't know.

The problem: automation & costs

Companies don't use AI because it's cool. They use it because of money. So we need to know if it's really cheaper, and who pays the price.

Why a company would pick AI

  • It works 24/7, no breaks, no sick days
  • It never asks for a raise or goes on strike
  • One program can do the work of a whole team
  • It gets cheaper every year

The hidden costs

  • Software, servers and electricity aren't free
  • You still need people to check it when it messes up
  • Customers sometimes hate talking to a bot
  • Someone has to retrain the workers who got replaced
  • What gets automated first?Tasks, not whole jobs. The boring, predictable parts go first: filling forms, answering the same question 200 times, sorting stuff.
  • Who pays to retrain people?The company? The government? The worker? Right now it feels like nobody has a clear answer, and that's kind of the problem.

AI workers · Case study: Zoe the teacher

Zoe teaches history at a high school. This year her school starts testing an AI tutor. Here's what a normal Tuesday looks like for her.

What the AI does better

  • Marks 90 tests in seconds
  • Explains the same topic 50 times without getting annoyed
  • Is there at 2 a.m. the night before the exam

What only Zoe can do

  • Notice a student is having a bad day
  • Control a class that's going crazy
  • Catch the AI when it makes stuff up with total confidence

The real question isn't "can the AI teach?" It's whether the school uses it to help Zoe, or to replace her with one teacher and a screen for 60 kids.

Zoe is a made-up example we used to think about the topic.

16:00

The future of the economy

This is the part nobody can answer for sure, but these are the things we need to find out.

Jobs that might shrink

  • Call center agents
  • Data entry & admin
  • Basic translation
  • Simple graphic design

Jobs that might appear

  • People who train & check AI
  • AI + human "hybrid" roles
  • Tech repair & maintenance
  • Jobs we can't even imagine yet
  • If companies save money, who keeps it?Just the owners, or does it reach regular people with lower prices, better salaries or shorter work weeks?
  • What should people my age study?Something AI can't easily copy, or something that teaches you to use AI really well. Ideally both.

OUT 18:00

So… will AI replace us?

Honestly? It worries me a bit. AI is moving crazy fast and nobody really knows where it's going. But after all this, I don't think it's going to replace everyone.

AI will probably replace parts of jobs, not whole people. And the people who know how to use AI will probably replace the people who don't.
